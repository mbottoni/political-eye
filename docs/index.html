political-eye

Brazilian politics and public money from official open data. Scheduled jobs download each source, rebuild tidy tables and commit them, so the repository's history is a record of what changed and when. Each page below goes from the big picture down to one deputy, party, company or town.

Every rule and data quirk: methodology · data and code: github.com/mbottoni/political-eye

Party orientations weekly

How often each deputy votes the way their party and the government told them to, and with their party's majority: government support by party, cohesion, rebels, how parties orient.

Deputies across sources weekly

Every deputy in one place: how they vote and whom they vote with, the amendment money they sent and where it landed, whether it favoured towns their party runs, and who paid for their campaign; then all of them compared.

Money: campaigns, amendments, contracts, towns

Campaign finance weekly

Where candidates' money came from in 2018, 2022 and 2026: how party leaderships split public funds, whether women and Black candidates got their legal share, how money relates to winning, the large donors and the companies campaigns paid.

TSE

Parliamentary amendments daily

Every amendment since 2014: who sent it, where the money landed, how much was committed and paid, and which organisations received it, "Pix" transfers included.

Portal da Transparência (CGU)

Public contracts daily

Who buys and who sells in contracts of R$1 million or more: supplier and buyer concentration, contracts with barred companies, money per inhabitant by state and town, and towns compared by their mayor's party. The history since 2021 is still being read.

PNCP

Municipal finances monthly

Which towns break the legal caps on payroll and which end the year without free cash, whether "Pix" amendments go to towns in trouble, each town's mayors, and towns compared by the mayor's party. The history since 2015 is still being read.

Sanctions daily

Who is barred from public contracts or fined for corruption, by whom and for how long: repeat offenders, fines, leniency deals, and the amendments and contracts barred companies still get, by member and party.
