Campaign finance

Companies have not been allowed to fund Brazilian campaigns since 2015. A candidate's money now comes mostly from public funds that party leaderships hand out, plus private individuals and the candidate's own pocket. This page follows that money in the 2018, 2022 and 2026 general elections: what each office raised and from where, how parties split their public money, whether women and Black candidates got their legal share, how money relates to winning, who the large donors are and whom campaigns paid. From the candidates' accounts published by the electoral court (TSE), refreshed weekly.

Money is what candidates declared they received, in reais of the election year. Public money means the Special Campaign Fund (FEFC) plus the Party Fund. "Black" follows the law's usage: candidates who declared themselves preta or parda. Incumbents are federal deputies who sat in the Câmara during the legislature that ends with the election, matched through the politicians crosswalk. The latest election's accounts are still being filed and judged, so its numbers change from week to week. Private donors are named only from R$50,000 in all, with their CPF masked; smaller donors appear only as counts. Rules and gaps: methodology/tse.md. Source: Tribunal Superior Eleitoral (TSE).
